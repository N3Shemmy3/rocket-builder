# Challenge context

## Design problem

The project is presented as a NASA Space Apps Challenge experience and links to the [Space Apps 2026 Space Mission Design Game resource page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/space-mission-design-game/?tab=resources). The problem Capricorn addresses is how to make mission planning and launch-vehicle trade-offs approachable and interactive: players have to fit a vehicle to a mission while working within cost, preparation, thrust, and delta-V limits.

## Capricorn's approach

Rather than expose a large engineering toolset, the game turns planning into a short, repeatable loop:

- Mission briefs provide a goal and explicit contract constraints.
- A fixed parts catalog makes vehicle configuration understandable.
- Live telemetry shows the consequences of each configuration.
- Surplus procurement offers a cost and schedule trade-off with a reliability penalty.
- A launch gate explains why an invalid configuration is not ready.
- A flight animation and campaign rewards provide a visible outcome.
- The NASA codex gives players additional context through orbital delta-V references, launch vehicle data, systems-engineering notes, and external resource links.

## What the challenge experience demonstrates

The game emphasizes that a mission is a system-level trade-off: meeting a payload or orbit target is not enough if the vehicle exceeds its budget or launch window. Comparing missions also shows how a change in objective affects the build. The codex and mission analogs encourage further exploration of real NASA programs and public resources.

## Boundaries

All five missions and their requirements are static game data. Parts, performance, and launch validation use simplified rules rather than real vehicle models, trajectory solvers, or live NASA datasets. The NASA comparisons and links are educational references; they should not be read as a certification, endorsement, or claim that the game reproduces official NASA mission planning.
